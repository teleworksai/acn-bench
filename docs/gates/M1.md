## What M1 delivers on mocks

- **The emulator** in `sim` and through the live proxy (SPEC 020), **the workload generator** (SPEC 050), **the control plane** (SPEC 070), **attribution** (SPEC 090) and **regeneration from a `run_id`** (SPEC 140), each with its acceptance suite.
- **A layered loop that was run end to end on the mock**: 1 548 `sim` bundles, a live twin of the cells the verdict rests on, and an evidence page rendered from the committed report, twin and verdicts.
- **A kit a second machine reproduced**: the tag `kit-m1`, 1 549 reference manifests, each regenerated byte for byte on other hardware from the manifest alone.

Nothing here is a result. Every verdict is `mock-gated` (CON-26).

## Known limitations carried past M1

- **The frozen verdict code is behind its spec.** The verdict crate (acn-hyp) follows HYP-22 as SPEC 080 v0.2 had it; v0.3 is merged as text. GATE-23's L2 verdict was made under v0.2, which is why it is `inconclusive` while every twinned quantity is within tolerance (ADR-46, ADR-50).
- **The loop of record ran on another build than the kit's.** It ran on `60d0899c…`, in the release profile; the kit's manifests are of the same bundles made again on the tag's build, each agreeing with the loop's in build-neutral form (ADR-52).
- **The kit's reference manifests are of one target**, `aarch64-apple-darwin`. Across targets only CI's reference run is compared (P16-12).
- **The second machine is a CI runner** (ADR-52), and the record it made is kept as a CI artifact for a limited time; the committed copy is what remains.
- **The live twin is slow**: 16 live bundles took most of a day on the served mock. Why was not investigated.
- **The mock is a model, with stated differences** (SPEC 100 P4-12; issues #26 and #32), as at M0.
